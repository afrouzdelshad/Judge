## Analysis

For these trajectories, a "lobe" shows up as an interval where the point oscillates radially **at a nearly fixed angle** θ = atan2(y,x), with r growing from ~1 up to ~20–25 and back. Transitions between lobes pass through the origin at small radius (so intermediate angles at tiny r don't count).

**Typical trajectory (e.g. B):** sustained large‑radius oscillations occur only at
θ ≈ −96°, −168°, +120°, +48°, −24° → 5 lobes, spacing 72°.

Spot‑checking A, C, D, E, F, G, H, I, J, K, M, … all show the same five directions (−168, −96, −24, 48, 120), with any intermediate angles occurring only while r ≲ 3 (i.e., during a hand‑off through the center).

**Trajectory L is different.** It shows full radial excursions (r up to ~20–25) at *twice as many* directions:
