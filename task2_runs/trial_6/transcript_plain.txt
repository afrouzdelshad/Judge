## Analysis Approach

Since the lobes are radial "petals" around the origin, I identified for each trajectory the **angular directions of its large‑radius excursions** (θ = atan2(y,x) at each radial maximum). The lobe axes show up as tight angular clusters.

**Typical trajectory (e.g. A):**
excursion angles cluster at ≈ 336°, 48°, 120°, 192°, 264° — five directions spaced ~72° apart, visited in retrograde order (192→120→48→336→264→…). So **N = 5**.

I verified this same set of five clusters {48, 120, 192, 264, 336} for B, C, D, E, F, G, H, I, J, K, M, N, O, P, Q, R, S, T. For instance:
- B: (17.4,21.7)=51°, (14.3,−6.7)=−25°, (−17.8,−4.7)=193°, (−12.2,21.4)=120°, (−2.1,−24.0)=−95°
- S: (17.3,18.7)=47°, (25.5,−11.0)=−23°, (−21.5,−3.2)=189°, (−12.7,20.6)=118°, (−2.3,−23.5)=−95°

**Trajectory L is different.** Its large excursions land on *ten* distinct directions spaced ~36°:
