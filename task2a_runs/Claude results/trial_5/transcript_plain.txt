## Analysis

For each trajectory I looked at where the orbit makes large‑radius excursions and computed the polar angle θ = atan2(y,x) of those excursions. For a system whose attractor has N lobes arranged around a central axis, these excursions cluster into N fixed angular directions (spaced 360°/N).

**Typical trajectory (e.g. G, T, M, N, O, P, Q, R, S, A–K):** the large‑radius points fall into exactly **five** angular clusters, always the same ones:

≈ 48°, 120°, 192°, 264° (−96°), 336° (−24°) — spacing ≈ 72° → **N = 5**

Example (T): −22°, +44°, +119°, +191°, −94°, repeated over and over.
