## Analysis Approach

For each trajectory I computed the polar angle θ = atan2(y,x) over time. In these systems the orbit "dwells" near a lobe (spiraling outward at a nearly fixed angular position) before hopping to the next lobe. The dwell angles form a set of equally spaced directions, and the angular step Δθ between successive lobes gives N = 360°/Δθ. I verified each by following a full 360° circuit and counting lobe visits.

**Examples:**
- **A**: dwell angles 180°, 135°, 90°, 45°, 0°, −45°, −90°, −135° → Δ = 45° → N = 8
- **S**: only two dwell regions, centered near (−8.3, −8.5) and (+6, +7) — classic two‑lobe (Lorenz‑like) → N = 2
- **G**: 48°, −12°, −72°, −132°, 168°, 108° → Δ = 60° → N = 6
- **C**: 66°, 45°, 25°, 4°, −18°, … stepping ≈21.2° per lobe, 17 visits per full turn → N = 17
- **L**: steps of ≈17.1°, 21 lobe visits from θ = 80° back to 80° → N = 21
- **N**: steps of ≈18.9°, 19 visits per revolution → N = 19
