## Approach

For each trajectory I computed the polar angle θ = atan2(y, x) at the points of large radius (where the orbit is circling a lobe center), and looked for the discrete set of preferred angles. In every case the preferred angles fell on a grid θ ≈ 45° + k·(360°/N), so counting the distinct angular clusters (or measuring the angular step between successive lobe visits) gives N.

**Examples:**
- **A**: clusters at 180°, 136°, 92°, 47°, 3°, −44°, −87°, −131° → step 45° → N = 8
- **B**: clusters at 194°, 121°, 48°, −23°, −96° → step 72° → N = 5
- **S**: only two large‑radius clusters, at ≈ +50° and ≈ −130° (dwell near (−9,−10), excursions near (+9,+10)) → N = 2
- **L**: 21 clusters (79.7, 63.1, 46.2, 28.6, 11, −6, −23, −41, −58, −76, −92, −109, −126, −144, −159, −178, 166, 148, 132, 115, 98) → step ≈17.1° → N = 21
- **N**: step ≈18.9° → N = 19; **C, Q**: step ≈21.2° → N = 17; **J**: step 22.5° → N = 16; **D**: step 25.7° → N = 14
