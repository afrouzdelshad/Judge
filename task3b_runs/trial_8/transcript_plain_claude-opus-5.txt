## Analysis Approach

For each trajectory I computed the polar angle θ = atan2(y, x) at the points of locally maximal radius (the lobe "tips"). In every trajectory these angles fall into a small set of sharply defined directions, and the spacing between adjacent directions gives N = 360°/Δθ directly.

Examples:

- **A**: tips at ≈ 2°, 48°, 93°, 138°, 183°, 227°, 272°, 317° → Δθ ≈ 45° → **N = 8**
- **C**: tips at ≈ 8°, 27°, 46°, 65°, 84°, 103°, 122°, 141°, 160°, 179°, … → Δθ ≈ 18.9° → **N = 19**
- **D**: Δθ ≈ 25.7° → **N = 14**;  **J**: Δθ ≈ 22.5° → **N = 16**
- **E/P/T**: Δθ ≈ 32.7° → **N = 11**;  **M/O**: Δθ ≈ 60° → **N = 6**
- **H/S**: only two broad spiral wings, centred ≈ 45° and ≈ 225° → **N = 2**

(Every attractor turns out to have one lobe near 46°, with the others evenly spaced by 360/N, which makes the identification unambiguous.)
