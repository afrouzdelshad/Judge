## Analysis approach

For each trajectory I converted (x,y) to a polar angle and tracked where the orbit "dwells" (repeated radial excursions at a nearly fixed angle) before jumping to the next dwell angle. The dwell angles always fall on an evenly‑spaced ring, and the angular step Δθ between successive lobes gives **N = 360°/Δθ**.

Representative measurements:

| Δθ (deg) | N | trajectories |
|---|---|---|
| ~180 | 2 | H, S (only two angular clusters, ≈ +52° and ≈ −128°) |
| ~60 | 6 | M, O (lobes at 168, 108, 48, −12, −72, −132) |
| ~45 | 8 | A, B, F, G, I, K, R (lobes at multiples of 45°) |
| ~32.7 | 11 | E, P, T (lobes 47, 15, −18, −50, −85, −117, −150, 178, 145, 111, 78) |
| ~25.7 | 14 | D |
| ~22.5 | 16 | J (−178, 159, 137, 114, 92, 69 …) |
| ~19.0 | 19 | C, L, N, Q |

**Consistency check:** the runs share only 7 distinct initial (x,y) values. In every group I obtained, each initial condition appears at most once (the N=8 group uses all 7 ICs, the N=19 group uses the 4 most common ICs, etc.), exactly as expected if the same seed points were integrated under each different‑N system. This nesting strongly corroborates the clustering.

Group sizes are 7, 4, 3, 2, 2, 1, 1 → X = 7, typical size ≈ 3.
