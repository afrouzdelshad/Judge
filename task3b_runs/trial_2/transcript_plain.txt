## Analysis Approach

For each trajectory I converted the (x,y) samples to polar angle θ and looked at the **large‑radius excursions**. Every trajectory dwells at a discrete set of angular directions ("lobes"), making radial in‑and‑out loops before hopping to the neighbouring lobe. The angular hop Δθ is constant within a trajectory, and one full revolution takes exactly 360/Δθ hops → that count is N.

Measured lobe spacings and lobe directions:

|---|---|---|---|
| 180° | +50, −130 | 2 | H, S |
| 60° | 48+60k | 6 | M, O |
| 45° | 2+45k | 8 | A, B, F, G, I, K, R |
| 32.7° | −18+32.7k | 11 | E, P, T |
| 25.7° | −4+25.7k | 14 | D |
| 22.5° | 1+22.5k | 16 | J |
| 18.95° | 8+19k | 19 | C, L, N, Q |

**Consistency check:** the t=0 points fall into only 7 distinct (x,y) seeds. Within each N‑class every trajectory uses a *different* seed, and the seed shared by 7 trajectories (0.591, 22.52) yields exactly one member of each of the seven classes (S→2, O→6, I→8, E→11, D→14, J→16, C→19). This independently confirms that there are 7 systems and that the classes above are correct (group sizes are unequal: 7,4,3,2,2,1,1).
