## Approach

Each trajectory consists of large radial "bursts." I computed the polar angle θ = atan2(y, x) at each burst peak. The bursts land on a discrete set of directions, and successive bursts advance by a fixed angular step Δθ. Unwrapping the angle sequence gives Δθ = 360°/N directly.

Examples:
- **O**: only two burst directions (≈ +55°, ≈ −130°) → step 180° → N = 2
- **A**: directions ≈ 50°, 172°, −70° → step 120° → N = 3
- **B**: 16°, −13°, −44°, −74°, −103°, … → step ≈ 30° → N = 12
- **T**: 46°, 28°, 12°, −6°, −23°, −39°, −57°, … → step ≈ 17.1° → N = 21
