## Approach

Since the trajectories orbit a central axis, the "lobes" appear as preferred angular directions θ = atan2(y, x) at which the orbit makes its large‑radius excursions. For each trajectory I collected the angles at points of large radius and looked for the angular lattice they fall on.

A useful check: all systems have a lobe at θ ≈ 45°, so the lobe set is θ = 45° + 360°k/N. The observed spacings came out cleanly:

| spacing | N | example angles seen |
|---|---|---|
| ~90° | 4 | 45, 135, 225, 315 |
| ~32.7° | 11 | 13, 46, 79, 111, 144, 177, 210, 243, 276, 308, 341 |
| ~22.5° | 16 | 1, 23, 45, 68, 91, 113, 136, 158, 180, … |
| ~18° | 20 | 9, 27, 45, 63, 81, 99, 117, 135, 153, 171, … |

**Consistency check:** the 20 runs use only 5 distinct initial conditions, each appearing 4 times — (15.25, 15.4): A, D, I, L; (0.59, 22.5): B, J, K, S; (−11.9, −10.1): C, E, G, H; (−20.7, −13.2): F, O, P, Q; (22.2, 0.57): M, N, R, T. Every group I found contains exactly one member from each IC family, which confirms the clustering.

- **N = 4** (angles wobble widely inside only 4 sectors): H, I, M, O, S
- **N = 11** (32.7° lattice): A, B, F, G, R
- **N = 16** (22.5° lattice): D, E, K, P, T
- **N = 20** (18° lattice): C, J, L, N, Q
